# History, positive correlations, and polarization: proofs

**Status:** the propositions in this note are proved under their stated hypotheses. They are applications or refinements of standard constructions, with no priority claim. Their application to an RH-sufficient arithmetic host remains open. Claim identifiers RMP-001–004 and RMP-007–008 are local to this checkpoint.

## 1. RMP-001: eliminating the environment produces memory

Let H_obs and H_env be Hilbert spaces and U a unitary on their orthogonal sum. Write

$$
U=\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\C&D\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\binom{x_{n+1}}{e_{n+1}}=U\binom{x_n}{e_n}.
$$

For arbitrary initial environment e_0, induction gives

$$
e_n=D^ne_0+\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}D^{n-1-j}Cx_j.
$$

Consequently

$$
\boxed{
x_{n+1}=Ax_n+BD^ne_0+
\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}BD^{n-1-j}Cx_j.}
\tag{1}
$$

The zero-environment version is the discrete memory formula in the overview. This identity does not require a probabilistic interpretation. It is a bounded-operator elimination identity.

If P projects onto H_obs, block inversion yields, for |z|<1,

$$
\boxed{
P(I-zU)^{-1}P
=
[I-zA-z^2B(I-zD)^{-1}C]^{-1}.}
\tag{2}
$$

Here both the left side and the right side act on H_obs. The assumptions ensure that I-zU and I-zD are invertible. The memory term contains complete excursions from the observed space into the environment and back.

### An exact finite falsifier

For sigma=+1 or -1, set

$$
U_\sigma=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&0&\sigma\\
1&0&0\\
0&1&0
\end{pmatrix},
\quad H_{\rm obs}=\operatorname{span}(e_0).
$$

Both matrices are orthogonal. For both, A=0. Moreover BC=0 but BDC=sigma. Hence a state starting at e_0 is invisible at steps 1 and2 and returns with amplitude sigma at step 3.

The repeated compressed map A^n predicts no return. The two systems have the same immediate shadow but different coherent histories. Erasing the environment at step 2 destroys the state; applying the inverse full dynamics to that erased state does not reconstruct e_0.

For a time-dependent word U_m...U_1, the inverse is U_1^*...U_m^*: the last operation is undone first. If an independently specified reversal involution Theta is used, reverse dynamics is Theta U^{-1}Theta^{-1}. None of these formulas licenses replacing a missing environmental state with its norm or with independent samples.

## 2. RMP-002: positive primitive erasure is constrained

### 2.1 Fixed unitaries force their products

**Proposition.** Let Phi:A->A be a unital completely positive map on a unital C*-algebra. If Phi(u)=u and Phi(v)=v for two unitaries u,v, then

$$
\Phi(uv^*)=uv^*.
$$

More generally it fixes every word in the fixed unitaries and their adjoints. Commutativity is unnecessary.

**Proof.** Use a Stinespring representation Phi(a)=V^*pi(a)V, with V an isometry. Since u and Phi(u) are unitary,

$$
V^*\pi(u)^*(I-VV^*)\pi(u)V
=\Phi(u^*u)-\Phi(u)^*\Phi(u)=0.
$$

The left side is X^*X, so pi(u)V=Vu. The same argument applies to u^*. It follows that Phi(ua)=uPhi(a) and Phi(au)=Phi(a)u for all a. Apply this with a=v^*, and iterate. This is the multiplicative-domain argument. Square.

**Scope.** This statement concerns full unitaries and a UCP observable map. It does not automatically apply to truncated shifts, refinement isometries, nonunital operations, or a nonlinear logarithm. Such alternatives must state their own positivity and recovery properties.

A quantitative version follows from Stinespring's covariance estimate. If ||Phi(u)-u||<=epsilon_u and ||Phi(v)-v||<=epsilon_v, then

$$
\|\Phi(uv^*)-uv^*\|
\le\epsilon_u+\epsilon_v+2\sqrt{\epsilon_u\epsilon_v}.
$$

Indeed the covariance term is bounded by the square root of
||I-Phi(u)Phi(u)^*|| times ||I-Phi(v)Phi(v)^*||, each at most twice the corresponding error; the product of the two images contributes the sum of the errors. Exact deletion therefore requires this upper bound to be at least1. See also [Rahaman, Theorems 1.1–1.2](https://arxiv.org/html/1701.06205v4) for the general multiplicative-domain criterion.

### 2.2 Critical half-density weights still prohibit complete mixed erasure

Let Gamma be the free abelian multiplicative group generated by distinct primes p,q. A normalized positive-definite function m on Gamma has positive-semidefinite Gram matrices

$$
K_{ij}=m(g_i^{-1}g_j).
$$

Assume

$$
m(1)=1,\quad m(p)=r,\quad m(q)=s,\quad
m(pq)=m(p/q)=0,
\qquad r,s\ge0.
$$

At g=(1,p,q,pq), the matrix is

$$
K=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&r&s&0\\
r&1&0&s\\
s&0&1&r\\
0&s&r&1
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{3}
$$

Its eigenvalues are 1+r+s, 1+r-s, 1-r+s, 1-r-s. Therefore r+s<=1 is necessary.

At p=2,q=3 and r=1/sqrt(2), s=1/sqrt(3),

$$
1-r-s=-0.284457050376\ldots<0.
$$

For w=(1,-1,-1,1),

$$
w^TKw=4(1-r-s)=-1.137828201504\ldots .
$$

This sign does not rely on floating-point eigenvalues. The rational inequalities r>7/10 and s>57/100 give the strict certificate

$$
w^TKw<-\frac{27}{25}.
$$

Every three-point principal submatrix of (3) is positive at these weights: its smallest eigenvalue is 1-sqrt(r²+s²)=1-sqrt(5/6)>0. Thus a test of only triples would miss this four-history obstruction.

For general mixed values a=m(pq), b=m(p/q), the same witness gives

$$
0\le4-4r-4s+2\operatorname{Re}(a+b),
$$

hence

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Re}(a+b)\ge2(r+s-1).}
\tag{4}
$$

At least one of |a|,|b| is at least r+s-1 whenever that number is positive. Both mixed channels cannot be made arbitrarily small while retaining the specified prime moments.

Conversely, m(p^kq^l)=r^{|k|}s^{|l|} is positive definite for 0<=r,s<1. The four-point matrix retains both mixed correlations rs and has minimum eigenvalue (1-r)(1-s)>0.

A consistent character phase twist m(g) -> chi(g)m(g) changes the Gram by diagonal unitary conjugation. It does not repair a negative eigenvalue.

### 2.3 Exact criterion for an entire axis-only mask

For finitely many prime axes indexed by j, choose 0<=r_j<1. Define m(0)=1, m(k e_j)=r_j^{|k|} for k!=0, and m=0 at all multi-axis lattice points. Its absolutely convergent Fourier series on the torus has density

$$
F(\theta)=1+\sum_j(P_{r_j}(\theta_j)-1),
\quad
P_r(\theta)=\frac{1-r^2}{1-2r\cos\theta+r^2}.
$$

The minimum of P_r is (1-r)/(1+r), attained at pi. Therefore

$$
\min F=1-\sum_j\frac{2r_j}{1+r_j}.
$$

A nonnegative density gives positive Grams by integration. Conversely, if the density is negative near a point, squared trigonometric polynomials concentrated there give a negative Gram quadratic form. Thus

$$
\boxed{
m\text{ is positive definite}
\iff
\sum_j\frac{2r_j}{1+r_j}\le1.}
\tag{5}
$$

At the half-density weights of primes 2 and3, the density minimum is approximately -0.560478. This is a stronger full-axis test than the finite square.

### 2.4 What this says about the Euler logarithm

The inherited Round003 identity is

$$
Z(x,y)=\frac1{(1-x)(1-y)},\qquad
\log Z=\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{x^k+y^k}{k}.
$$

The xy state is present in Z, and absent from log Z. The new positive-map obstruction says that this connected extraction cannot in general be reinterpreted as a positive map that erases every composite/ratio state while preserving the prime observable channels.

The vanishing of Lambda(6) is a statement about an independent connected source. It is not the disappearance of the history 2 followed by3. This distinction is essential for inverse histories: a ratio such as 3/2 can be encountered by reverse/forward composition even though it is not an independent prime source.

## 3. RMP-003: exact duality and exact reversal can coexist with off-center eigenvalues

Let B be an invertible real alternating matrix and D a real matrix. A weight-one bilinear duality for the generator D is

$$
D^TB+BD=B.
\tag{6}
$$

It implies e^{tD^T}Be^{tD}=e^tB. It does not imply the preservation of a positive norm.

For example,

$$
D_{\rm bad}=\begin{pmatrix}3/4&0\\0&1/4\end{pmatrix},
\quad
B=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Equation (6) holds. Put R=[[0,1],[1,0]] and A=D_bad-1/2 I. Then RAR=-A, hence

$$
R e^{tA}R=e^{-tA}.
$$

There is a precise reversal identity. Nevertheless the eigenvalues of D_bad are 3/4 and1/4.

**Positive-polarization proposition.** Suppose, in addition to (6), that

$$
J^2=-I,\quad G=BJ=G^T>0,\quad DJ=JD.
\tag{7}
$$

Then

$$
D^TG+GD=(D^TB+BD)J=G.
$$

Thus A=D-1/2 I satisfies A^TG+GA=0. Conjugating by G^{1/2} makes A an ordinary real skew-symmetric matrix. All eigenvalues of D therefore have real part1/2, and

$$
e^{tA^T}G e^{tA}=G.
$$

For D_bad a positive G is impossible: its e_1 diagonal equation would require (3/2)G_11=G_11, so G_11=0. The positive control

$$
D_{\rm good}=\begin{pmatrix}1/2&-3\\3&1/2\end{pmatrix},
\quad J=-B,\quad G=I
$$

satisfies all the requirements.

This finite-dimensional calculation distinguishes a duality datum from a polarization compatible with the dynamics. Deninger's conjectural cohomological formalism makes the same separation through cup product, trace, and an additional positive Hodge-star operator; see the source audit in the companion note.

For infinite-dimensional operators, replace matrix equations with identities on explicit domains. Skew-symmetry on a core does not alone establish skew-adjointness, a unitary group, or completeness of the proposed spectral realization.

## 4. RMP-004: finite-field reversal has a positive common history

### 4.1 The exact intersection model

Let C_0/F_q be smooth projective geometrically connected of genus g>=1, and C its base change to an algebraic closure. On C×C use H=C×{P}, V={P}×C, diagonal Delta, and the graph Gamma_n of q^n-Frobenius.

Their intersections satisfy

$$
H^2=V^2=0,\quad H\cdot V=1,\quad
\Gamma_n\cdot H=q^n,\quad \Gamma_n\cdot V=1,
$$

$$
\Gamma_n^2=q^n(2-2g),\quad
\Delta\cdot\Gamma_n=N_n=\#C_0(F_{q^n}).
$$

The graph self-intersection follows from its normal bundle; the fixed-point intersection is simple because the derivative of Frobenius is zero. Define

$$
D_0=\Delta-H-V,\qquad
D_n=\Gamma_n-H-q^nV.
$$

Both are perpendicular to the ample class H+V. The Hodge index theorem makes minus intersection positive semidefinite there. Expanding gives

$$
-\begin{pmatrix}D_0^2&D_0D_n\\D_0D_n&D_n^2\end{pmatrix}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
2g&q^n+1-N_n\\
q^n+1-N_n&2gq^n
\end{pmatrix}\succeq0.
\tag{8}
$$

The determinant inequality is the Hasse–Weil bound. Its validity for every n, together with rationality and the functional equation of the curve zeta function, gives the finite-field RH.

These are classical ingredients; primary exposition: Milne, The Riemann Hypothesis over Finite Fields, Theorem 1.2, Theorem 1.5, Corollary 1.6, Example 1.7. The inequalities are not supplied by reversing a value: they are supplied by an ample class and the Hodge index theorem.

### 4.2 Rosati turns the transpose into a positive adjoint

Let J_C be the Jacobian with its canonical F_q-polarization lambda. On its rational endomorphism algebra define

$$
a^\dagger=\lambda^{-1}a^\vee\lambda.
$$

For Frobenius pi,

$$
\pi^\dagger\pi=\pi\pi^\dagger=q,
\quad
\tau(aa^\dagger)>0\quad(a\ne0),
$$

where tau is the rational Tate-module trace. See Milne, Abelian Varieties, I§14 Theorem 14.3 and II§1 Lemmas 1.2–1.3.

In E=Q[pi] tensor R, use the positive form (x,y)=tau(xy^\dagger), normalized left multiplication Ux=q^{-1/2}pi x, and R_E(x)=x^\dagger. Then U is orthogonal and

$$
R_EUR_E=U^{-1}.
$$

We restrict to this commutative, dagger-stable algebra: on the full noncommutative endomorphism algebra, dagger exchanges left and right multiplication. This E is an endomorphism-trace model, not a claim to have constructed a real Weil cohomology.

Curve Frobenius is inseparable and has no inverse as a curve morphism. The Jacobian Frobenius pi is invertible in End^0(J_C), with pi^{-1}=q^{-1}pi^\dagger. The polarization makes its normalized transpose an adjoint.

### 4.3 Several times must fit the same pairing

Put a_0=2g and a_n=q^n+1-N_n. Set c_0=a_0=2g; for n>0 set c_n=q^{-n/2}a_n and c_{-n}=c_n. From the preceding model,

$$
c_{j-i}=(U^i1,U^j1).
$$

Every Toeplitz matrix (c_{j-i}) is positive semidefinite. Equivalently, the unnormalized Gram is

$$
\boxed{
G_{ij}=q^{\min(i,j)}a_{|i-j|}\succeq0.}
\tag{9}
$$

This is a consequence of the positive Rosati pairing. It is stronger as a finite consistency test than checking individual Hasse inequalities without checking their common origin.

The probe counts y²=x³−4 independently over F_p and F_p² for p=5,7,11,13,17,19,23,29,31. Brute point counts and quadratic-character counts agree. All three-time integer Grams have nonnegative principal minors.

For the synthetic q=5,g=2 polynomial

$$
P(X)=X^4+6X^3+27X^2+30X+25,
$$

one has X^4P(5/X)=25P(X), a_1=-6, a_2=-18, N_1=12,N_2=44. These counts also give a nonnegative integer degree-two closed-point count 16. Both individual Hasse bounds hold. But

$$
G=\begin{pmatrix}
4&-6&-18\\
-6&20&-30\\
-18&-30&100
\end{pmatrix},
\quad \det G=-12160.
$$

Its two-by-two principal minors are 44, 76, 1100, all positive. For w=(4,1,1), w^TGw=-68. Reciprocal symmetry and pairwise tests therefore do not produce a positive common history. The polynomial is a deliberate formal negative control, not the zeta polynomial of an asserted curve.

## 5. RMP-007: a complete reversible environment for the known positive energy

This section constructs an environment for the exact positive Gamma/prime energies. It does not construct an environment whose leakage equals the full Weil form.

Fix A>0, H_A=L²(-A,A), and extend functions by zero to R. Write

$$
a_n=\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n},\qquad
w_\Gamma(h)=\frac{e^{-h/2}}{1-e^{-2h}}\quad(h>0),
$$

$$
\nu_A(dh)=w_\Gamma(|h|)\,dh+
\sum_{2\le n\le e^{2A}}a_n(\delta_{\log n}+\delta_{-\log n})(dh).
$$

On the natural closed form domain define the difference map

$$
(\nabla_A f)(h,x)=\frac{f(x)-f(x-h)}{\sqrt2}
$$

with target K_A=L²(nu_A(dh);L²(R,dx)). Its exact energy is

$$
\|\nabla_Af\|^2=
E_\Gamma[f]+E_{{\rm prime},A}[f]=:\mathcal E_A[f].
\tag{10}
$$

The associated self-adjoint operator L_A=nabla_A^*nabla_A is positive. The Gamma tail gives

$$
L_A\ge c_AI,\qquad
c_A=2\int_{2A}^\infty w_\Gamma(h)\,dh>0.
$$

The underlying energy identity and closed domain are inherited from the prior succ–Weil–Suzuki branch. We now apply explicit dilation constructions.

### 5.1 Exact two-channel recovery

Let L=L_A, T_w=e^{-wL}, R_w=(I-e^{-2wL})^{1/2}. Then

$$
\mathcal U_w=
\begin{pmatrix}T_w&-R_w\\R_w&T_w\end{pmatrix}
$$

is unitary, since the entries commute and their squared sum is I. It maps (f,0) to (T_wf,R_wf). If g=T_wf and eta=R_wf, recovery is

$$
f=T_wg+R_w\eta,\qquad -R_wg+T_w\eta=0.
$$

The environmental vector, not just its norm, is needed.

The spectral theorem gives

$$
\frac{\|f\|^2-\|T_wf\|^2}{2w}\longrightarrow\mathcal E_A[f],
\quad
\frac{R_wf}{\sqrt{2w}}\longrightarrow L^{1/2}f
\tag{11}
$$

on the form domain. Outside that domain the first limit is infinite. The scalar identity (1-e^{-2wlambda})/(2w) increasing to lambda proves these claims by spectral convergence.

This family consists of dilations, not necessarily a group. A genuine rotation group is

$$
V_\tau=
\begin{pmatrix}
\cos(\tau\sqrt L)&-\sin(\tau\sqrt L)\\
\sin(\tau\sqrt L)&\cos(\tau\sqrt L)
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Using tau=sqrt(2w) gives the same energy tangent. A smooth clock tau=w instead gives zero after division by 2w on the finite-energy domain.

### 5.2 A genuine time-translation group with a prepared history

There is a construction that uses an ordinary translation clock. Define

$$
(\mathcal I f)(s)=
\sqrt2\,\nabla_Ae^{sL}f\,1_{s\le0}
$$

in B_A=L²(R_s;K_A). For all f in H_A, spectral integration gives

$$
\|\mathcal I f\|^2=
2\int_0^\infty\mathcal E_A[e^{-tL}f]\,dt=\|f\|^2.
$$

Strict positivity of L removes a kernel component. Thus I is an isometry.

Let (W_tF)(s)=F(s-t), a strongly continuous unitary group on B_A. For t>=0,

$$
\boxed{\mathcal I^*W_t\mathcal I=e^{-tL}.}
\tag{12}
$$

Indeed, the inner product of two prepared histories after shifting reduces to the preceding spectral integral with an extra factor e^{-tL}.

There is an explicit orthogonal decomposition

$$
W_t\mathcal I f=\mathcal I(e^{-tL}f)+\eta_t,
$$

$$
\eta_t(s)=
\sqrt2\,\nabla_Ae^{-(t-s)L}f\,1_{0<s\le t}.
\tag{13}
$$

The summands have disjoint history-time supports, and

$$
\|\eta_t\|^2=\|f\|^2-\|e^{-tL}f\|^2.
$$

Full recovery applies W_{-t} to both summands and then I^*. Erasing eta_t removes exactly ||eta_t||^2 from the full history. After reversal and projection, the recovered observed vector is e^{-2tL}f, rather than f. The record retains displacement h, position x, history time s, and the coherent amplitudes.

This provides a mathematically literal version of assembling the earlier observed state from pieces distributed through an environment.

### 5.3 Why linear leakage does not contradict the smooth-clock obstruction

If Q projects away from an initially observed vector if, and U(t)if is differentiable at 0, then

$$
QU(t)if=tQU'(0)if+o(t).
$$

Its squared norm is O(t²), so a nonzero first-order leakage rate is impossible under that hypothesis.

In the history construction, for nonzero finite-energy f the prepared function has left trace sqrt(2)nabla_A f at s=0 and right trace0. Its derivative contains a delta contribution and is not in L². It lies outside the domain of the translation generator. The full group exists, but the prepared state is not differentiable under it.

This identifies the singularity explicitly; “the generator is unbounded” alone would not explain it on vectors in its domain.

Likewise, bounded one-step loss does not imply a bounded first variation. For unbounded L,

$$
0\le I-e^{-2wL}\le I,\qquad
\left\|\frac{I-e^{-2wL}}{2w}\right\|=\frac1{2w}.
$$

An unbounded form tangent is compatible with bounded loss at every fixed positive w.

### 5.4 Gamma is a legitimate continuum environment; the full uncentered prime measure is not

Near zero, w_Gamma(h) is asymptotic to1/(2h); at infinity it decays exponentially. Thus the symmetric Gamma measure has infinite total mass but satisfies

$$
\int(1\wedge h^2)\,\nu_\Gamma(dh)<\infty.
$$

Its infinite activity consists of small jumps. Its exact characteristic exponent is

$$
\alpha(u)
=2\sum_{j\ge0}\frac{u^2}{b_j(b_j^2+u^2)},
\quad b_j=2j+\tfrac12,
$$

$$
\alpha(u)=\operatorname{Re}\psi(1/4+iu/2)-\psi(1/4).
$$

Integral comparison of the decreasing summand gives

$$
\frac12\log(1+4u^2)\le\alpha(u)
\le\frac12\log(1+4u^2)+\frac{4u^2}{u^2+1/4}.
$$

This is an exact logarithmic-growth bound.

By contrast, removing the cutoff from the positive prime measure gives infinite mass at large displacements:

$$
\sum_p\frac{\log p}{\sqrt p}\ge\sum_{p\ge3}\frac1p=\infty.
$$

The divergence of the prime reciprocal sum follows, for example, because a convergent prime reciprocal sum would make the Euler product at 1 finite while its finite products dominate arbitrarily long harmonic sums. Therefore the full prime measure fails the Levy integrability condition. Small-jump compensation cannot repair infinite mass at large jumps.

For nonzero compactly supported f, all sufficiently large shifts have disjoint support, so every squared difference equals 2||f||². The full uncentered positive prime energy is infinite.

The actual Weil form uses the cancellation

$$
E_{{\rm prime},A}[f]-2M_A\|f\|^2
=-2\sum_{n\le e^{2A}}a_n\operatorname{Re}(f*\widetilde f)(\log n),
\quad M_A=\sum_{n\le e^{2A}}a_n.
$$

With C_f=(f,cosh(t/2)), S_f=(f,sinh(t/2)) and
d_A=log(pi)-psi(1/4)+2M_A, the exact target is

$$
\boxed{
Q_W(f)=\mathcal E_A[f]+2|C_f|^2
-\bigl(d_A\|f\|^2+2|S_f|^2\bigr).}
\tag{14}
$$

The positive C channel can be added to the dilation. Domination of the bracket remains unproved. A source-derived contractive dilation whose first variation were the full Q_W would solve the missing sign problem; (10)–(13) establish only the stated positive-energy part.

The earlier [full Suzuki–Weil tangent theorem](https://github.com/femboy2112/FuckingRH/blob/e6f2d08716cc699efec10eafd6738e770615bc76/research/aletheia_2026-10-07/succ_weil_suzuki/FULL_SUZUKI_WEIL_TANGENT.md) establishes, for each fixed compact smooth test v,

\lim_{\omega\downarrow0}
\frac{\|v\|^2-\|H_{\omega,A}v\|^2}{2\omega}

\tag{15}
$$

This is a form tangent, not operator-norm convergence. The Suzuki family is not identified with the heat semigroup constructed above: (11) yields the positive energy, while (15) yields the full form with its subtractions.

Building a block dilation by inserting sqrt(I-H_omega,A^2) assumes the sign one must prove; it is not a construction of the missing arithmetic environment. The independently sourced gradient model above supplies a valid partial environment and locates exactly what it does not yet reproduce.

## 6. RMP-008: ordinary L² closure is not yet spectral cohomology

**Proposition.** Let m be finite and nonzero almost everywhere, and let M_m be maximal multiplication by m on L²(R). Then

$$
\ker M_m=0,\qquad
\overline{\operatorname{Ran}M_m}=L^2.
$$

**Proof.** For f in L² put

$$
E_N=\{|u|\le N,\ 1/N\le|m(u)|\le N\},
\quad g_N=1_{E_N}f/m.
$$

Then g_N is square integrable, belongs to the maximal domain, and M_mg_N=1_{E_N}f tends to f in L². The kernel claim is immediate. Square.

For m(u)=zeta(1/2+iu), finiteness on this line and isolated zeros give the hypotheses without RH. Therefore its reduced Hilbert cokernel is

$$
L^2/\overline{\operatorname{Ran}M_m}=0.
$$

The algebraic quotient by the unclosed range can be nonzero and non-Hausdorff; it is a different object.

The imported arithmetic–Poisson construction identifies its closed Muntz operator with this maximal multiplication. Conditional on that donor identification, the proposition shows why ordinary L² closure cannot itself supply a nontrivial zero-carrying Hilbert cohomology. The donor already warns against interpreting multiplier zeros as eigenvalues; this is the explicit cokernel consequence.

A proposed dualizing/cohomological host must state which analytic topology, domains, or different quotient retain the needed data, and then prove that its positive pairing is well defined there. Closing a source operator and invoking an abstract quotient is insufficient.
