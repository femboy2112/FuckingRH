# From crests to the exact CND seam: paid identities and blocked maps

**RH is not proved.** The common positive moment mechanisms and the direct
radical identification are refuted below. No finite-wavefront Gram map with
a construction-derived uniform lift was obtained.

## 1. Fix the exact horizon and kernel

Retain the frozen Suzuki A, P, Psi=A-P and the anchored kernel
`K_F(s,u)=F(s)+F(u)-F(s-u)`. For |t|<=L, all active events p^k<=e^L
belong to primes p<=e^L. Complete-tower grouping gives exactly

\[
 \Psi(t)=A(t)+\sum_{p\le e^L}D_p(t)-M_L|t|,
 \quad M_L=\sum_{p\le e^L}\frac{\log p}{\sqrt p-1}.
\]

Thus on [0,L] the requested seam is exact. It keeps full towers, including
their inactive tail repair, while their ramps outside the visible horizon
do not contribute. Complexity labels neither move nor replace that horizon.
Round 002's independent interval Gram map proves every D_p CND. It does not
prove A is CND or that their sum dominates M_L K_|.|.

## 2. Uniform lift is sufficient, and remains RH-equivalent

Suppose for every L there is c_L in [0,C], with one finite C, such that
`K_Psi+c_L B` is PSD on every finite subset of [0,L], B=K_|.|. Because B
is PSD, adding `(C-c_L)B` shows that the **same** coefficient C works on
every horizon. No compactness/subsequence choice is actually needed.

This gives PSD of K_(Psi+C|.|) on the positive half-line. To pass to CND
on all real points, shift any finite zero-sum coefficient configuration by
minus its smallest point. Distances do not change, and its K quadratic
form is the negative of the distance quadratic form. The shift puts every
point in some positive horizon. Therefore Psi+C|.| is globally CND.

A continuous normalized CND function is O(1+t^2): its square root is the
distance of a stationary Hilbert increment, so the triangle inequality and
a bound on [0,1] give quadratic growth. Hence Psi has polynomial growth.
The frozen arithmetic identity

\[
 \int_0^\infty\Psi(t)e^{-st}\,dt
 =\frac1{s^2}\frac{\xi'}{\xi}(1/2+s)
 \quad(\Re s>1/2)
\]

then extends its left side holomorphically to Re(s)>0. This excludes poles
of the logarithmic derivative there; functional symmetry yields RH. This
is the Round-001 growth lemma, not a construction from zero ordinates.
Conversely RH gives c_L=0 by Suzuki. The uniform-lift target therefore
has exactly RH's strength; it is not a new weaker criterion.

## 3. One function direction is not one Hilbert mode

At ordered times 0<t_1<...<t_n, put d_i=t_i-t_(i-1). The Brownian matrix
factors exactly as `B=2 C diag(d_i) C^T`, where C is the unit lower
triangular cumulative-sum matrix. Its determinant is
`2^n product d_i>0`. The scalar coefficient M_L multiplies a rank-n matrix
on every n-point grid. As a continuous kernel this is infinite rank.

Consequently an orthogonal Hilbert quotient removing a fixed r-dimensional
subspace cannot subtract a nonzero Brownian term on all grids: the removed
Gram matrix has rank at most r, whereas M_L B has rank n for n>r.
This is a scoped no-go for finite-dimensional nulling, not for every
possible infinite-dimensional primitive quotient. Calling the debt one
common function-space direction does not pay its covariance rank.

Moreover quotienting functions by R|t| does not give a pointed positive
cone. Both signs of the nonzero tower class [h_p] have CND representatives,
by the two sharp Round-002 repairs. Thus positivity of separate quotient
classes cannot define an order that dominates the debt. A specific lift
with a controlled coefficient remains necessary.

## 5. Boundary of the no-go, and the smaller unresolved object

No theorem here excludes every nonlinear/compressed arithmetic construction.
A survivor must be an **explicit positive operator with nontrivial cross
terms**, not a common positive scalar mixture of old tower observables.
Its map must create or relocate the missing p-power spectral contribution,
which the ordinary grading-preserving Dirichlet logarithm cannot do. It must
also include infinity in the same metric and calculate the residual Brownian
coefficient before any eigenvalue fit.

The support theorem is for old-generated positive integers and their ordinary
Dirichlet algebra. Allowing ratios of old integers changes the issue: logs
of the group generated by 2 and 3 are dense, so the discrete-support closure
argument no longer applies to arbitrary limits of such frequencies. A
claimed extension to that larger space would need a new topology and norm
bound. Density alone constructs neither the Suzuki marginal nor positivity.

The precise next finite compatibility problem is now: give an actual
one-prime-plus-infinity operator/test-vector map whose full Gram pullback
contains the required p-tower atoms with the correct sign and whose difference
from K_Psi is a calculable multiple of B. The known canonical ambient Sonin
map was evaluated and fails the proposed simple norm-increment identity.
Any alternative must state its domain, metric, adjoint and cross terms.
An assertion of “primitive projection” without that map does not discharge
an arrow. Even a successful one-prime construction would still owe a uniform
bound across horizons.

## 6. Finite calibration cannot supply the missing map

Thirteen selected actual grids have certified zero lift cost; six mutations
need positive cost. The grid instrument also has a deliberately invisible
negative tail. It therefore detects some wrong arithmetic while proving
only finite matrix positivity. No coefficient in the proposed all-horizon
Gram construction has been defined by those eigenvalue calculations.
There is no arrow from those samples to this section's uniform theorem.
