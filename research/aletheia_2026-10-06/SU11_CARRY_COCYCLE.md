## 2. Fixed \(2\times2\) transfer group

Represent \(\varphi_\alpha\) projectively by

\[
\boxed{
M_\alpha
=
\frac1{\sqrt{1-|\alpha|^2}}
\begin{pmatrix}
1&\alpha\\
\bar\alpha&1

Let

\[
J=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&0\\
0&-1
\end{pmatrix}.

Then

\[
\boxed{
M_\alpha^*JM_\alpha=J,
\qquad
\det M_\alpha=1.
}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
M_\alpha\in SU(1,1).
}
\]

So every local prime phase, no matter its angle, lives in the same structure-preserving transfer group.

Let

\[
\mathcal C(z)=i\frac{1+z}{1-z}

\[

\frac{am+b}{cm+d}

\[
\boxed{

\mathcal C^{-1}SL(2,\mathbb R)\mathcal C

Every chronological prime/carry product therefore acts by an automorphism of the upper half-plane.

## 5. Herglotz class is invariant under the cocycle

Suppose

\[
m(z)
\]

is Herglotz:

\[
\Im z>0
\Longrightarrow
\Im m(z)>0.
\]

For a real matrix

\[
T=
\begin{pmatrix}
a&b\\
c&d
\end{pmatrix}
\in SL(2,\mathbb R),
\]

set

\[
T\cdot m
=
\frac{am+b}{cm+d}.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\Im(T\cdot m)
=
\frac{
\Im m
}{
|cm+d|^2

>0.
}
\]

Thus real transfer matrices preserve the Weyl/Herglotz class exactly.

Also, \(cm+d\) cannot vanish in \(\mathbb C_+\), because that would require \(m=-d/c\in\mathbb R\), impossible for a strict Herglotz function.

Therefore the transfer update cannot manufacture an upper-half-plane pole from a valid Weyl state.
