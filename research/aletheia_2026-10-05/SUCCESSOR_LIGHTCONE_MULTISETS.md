## 1. Two canonical free structures on the naturals

There are two exceptionally simple algebraic descriptions of positive integers.

### Additive substrate

\[
(\mathbb N,+,0)
\]

is the free commutative monoid on one generator \(1\). Equivalently,

\[
n=S^n(0),\qquad S(n)=n+1.
\]

Its Cayley graph with generator \(S\) is the ray

\[
0\to1\to2\to3\to\cdots.
\]

This gives a canonical causal order:

\[
m\preceq n
\iff
\exists k\ge0:\ n=S^k(m).
\]

Thus successor is the primitive forward-propagation operator of the additive substrate.

On \(\ell^2(\mathbb N)\), the unilateral shift

\[
Ue_n=e_{n+1}
\]

is an isometry and positivity-preserving on nonnegative coordinate functions, although it is not a positive self-adjoint operator in the C*-algebraic sense. The physically useful statement is therefore "canonical forward shift / causal propagator," not "positive operator" without qualification.

## 2. Multiplicative occupation space

Unique factorization gives

\[
\mathbb N_{>0}^{\times}
\cong
\bigoplus_{p\ {\rm prime}}\mathbb N e_p.
\]

An integer

\[
n=\prod_pp^{v_p(n)}
\]

is a finite occupation vector

\[
(v_2(n),v_3(n),v_5(n),\ldots).
\]

Thus multiplicative arithmetic is the free commutative monoid / bosonic multiset space generated by prime modes.

This is exactly the old powerset/multiset picture:

- squarefree support is the Boolean/powerset floor;
- multiplicities attach occupation fibers over that support;
- prime powers are repeated occupation of one primitive mode;
- composites are tensor/multiset states built from several modes.

## 3. Zeta as the partition function of the full positive state space

The Euler product

\[
\zeta(s)
=
\prod_p(1-p^{-s})^{-1}
\]

expands as

\[
\zeta(s)=\sum_{n\ge1}n^{-s}.
\]

This is the partition function of the full multiplicative occupation space.

Each local factor

\[
(1-p^{-s})^{-1}
=
1+p^{-s}+p^{-2s}+\cdots
\]

is the occupation ladder of one prime mode.

So the full composite state space is not discarded arithmetic junk. It is the natural positive/free state space upstairs.

## 4. Powerset floor and multiplicity fibers

The old finite-prime powerset/multiset decomposition can be restated as follows.

The squarefree radical

\[
\operatorname{rad}(n)=\prod_{p\mid n}p
\]

records the Boolean support set of occupied prime modes.

The multiplicity vector

\[
(v_p(n)-1)_{p\mid n}
\]

records the excess occupation attached to that support.

Hence integers are organized as

\[
\boxed{
\text{finite prime powerset}
+
\text{multiplicity fiber}.
}
\]

This is the exact free-commutative-monoid geometry underlying the prior multiset construction.

## 5. Connected / primitive projection

Taking logarithms of the partition function gives

\[
\log\zeta(s)
=
\sum_p\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{p^{-ks}}k.
\]

The logarithm removes disconnected multiplicative assembly and retains primitive prime cycles together with repetitions.

Differentiating gives

\[
-\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(s)
=
\sum_{p,k\ge1}
(\log p)p^{-ks}.
\]

Thus the hierarchy is

\[
\boxed{
\zeta
\to
\log\zeta
\to
-\partial_s\log\zeta
}
\]

or, conceptually,

\[
\boxed{
\text{all positive composite/multiset states}
\to
\text{connected/primitive cycles}
\to
\text{weighted primitive dynamics}.
}
\]

This is the precise version of:

> composites may be the positive state space upstairs; prime powers are what remains after the connected/primitive projection.

## 7. Successor as arithmetic light cone

The light-cone analogy should be used structurally, not literally.

At state \(n\), the future generated by repeated successor is

\[
J^+(n)=\{n,n+1,n+2,\ldots\},
\]

and the past is

\[
J^-(n)=\{0,1,\ldots,n\}.
\]

There is one primitive forward edge from each state:

\[
n\to n+1.
\]

Higher operators do not create new reachable values; they compress or reorganize successor histories.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{successor determines causal reachability;}
\quad
+\,,\times,\exp,\ldots
\text{ determine compressed histories within that causal substrate.}
}
\]
