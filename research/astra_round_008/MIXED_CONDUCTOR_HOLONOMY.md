# Mixed conductor phases: what the clock forces

Equations (B3)–(B4) calculate the phases through a new digit. They are additive
characters: the old coordinate is chirped by `exp(-2*pi*i*r*a/(pL))`, and
the digit has character `exp(-2*pi*i*r*b/p)`. CRT recombines their indices
as `h=M c+p^k a`. Ramanujan sums result from summing the primitive additive
characters in an exact conductor projection. No multiplicative character or
quadratic Gauss sum has appeared in this derivation.

For two refinements leading to the same final modulus, the canonical Haar
pullbacks are identical maps: pull back a function modulo the initial length.
Permuting CRT tensor coordinates changes the coordinate presentation, not
the final residue function or clock. Thus the refinement square is flat up
to its explicit coordinate permutation. It does not force a quadratic
reciprocity phase. Choosing additional Fourier transforms, chirps, or
multiplicative-character projections can introduce such sums, but then the
extra operations and the new observable must be derived separately.

The birth-prime spectral charge *is* history-sensitive. The 2-then-3 and
3-then-2 refinements end at the same clock `C_6` but attach different charges
to different innovation subspaces. Their full products at `s=2` are
`(1+2^-2)(1+3^-4+3^-8)` and `(1+3^-2+3^-4)(1+2^-6)`, respectively, and
are unequal rational numbers. This is an actual, exactly tested dependence
on the event ordering, not a hidden phase of the uncharged clock.

Keeping every mixed conductor is essential for a correct finite Fourier
interface. It is not sufficient for the desired Weil energy. The original
clock is simultaneously diagonal on all conductor harmonics; a shared
origin metric or Fourier/real comb is an additional interaction. We compute
those interactions before taking determinants, so their residuals cannot be
hidden by prematurely replacing all sectors with scalar Euler factors.
