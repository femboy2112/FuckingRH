## 1. The function-field rung, completely

For a smooth projective curve `C/F_q` of genus `g` with `N_m = #C(F_(q^m))` and
inverse zeros `α_1, …, α_2g`, the **explicit formula** is the identity

```
S_m := Σ_i α_i^m  =  (q^m + 1)  −  N_m,     N_m = Σ_{d | m} d · P_d,
```

where `P_d` is the number of closed points of degree `d` (Möbius inversion
recovers `P_d` from the `N`'s). Its two sides have different **hosts**:

| term | host (in the vocabulary of A) | provenance |
|---|---|---|
| `q^m + 1` | the global function field, its trivial cohomology `H^0`, `H^2` (the factor `(1−u)(1−qu)`) | "pole terms" |
| `d·P_d`, `d | m` | the family of all closed points (all finite places; each has an integral default) | "prime side" |
| — | (no archimedean place: the global host has **no mandatory points**) | — |

Put `z_i = α_i/√q` and `σ_m = S_m q^(−m/2)`. Because (+)-self-duality makes the
multiset `{z_i}` closed under `z ↦ 1/z`, `σ_{−m} = σ_m`, and the **curve Weil
form** on polynomials `f(z) = Σ_{k<n} x_k z^k` is

```
W_n(x) = Σ_i f(z_i) f(1/z_i) = Σ_{k,l} x_k x_l σ_{|k−l|}      (an n×n Toeplitz form).
```

The integer matrix `M_{ij} = q^{min(i,j)} S_{|i−j|}` equals `D T D` with
`T_{ij} = σ_{|i−j|}` and `D = diag(q^{i/2})`, so it is congruent to the Toeplitz
form and has the same inertia; Hallouin–Perret normalise the same matrix as
`T(1, x_1, x_2, …)`, `x_i = S_i/(2g q^{i/2})`.

**Proposition 1 (Proved here).** For (+)-self-dual `P` of degree `2g`:
`P` is pure ⟺ `W_{2g} ⪰ 0`. More precisely the negative index of `W_{2g}` is
`#(real off-circle pairs {r, 1/r}) + 2·#(non-real off-circle quadruples {z, z̄, 1/z, 1/z̄})`.

*Proof.* Group the distinct `z_i` into orbits of `z ↦ 1/z` and conjugation. By
real interpolation (THEOREM.md Lemma 4, with `n = 2g ≥` the number of distinct
`z_i`) the values `f(z_i)` at distinct points are independent coordinates, so the
inertia of `W_{2g}` is the sum of the inertias of the orbit forms (plus a kernel
for repeated roots). On-circle pair `{z, z̄}` (`z̄ = 1/z`): `2μ|f(z)|²`, signature
`(2,0)`; `z = ±1`: `μ f(±1)²`, `(1,0)`; real pair `{r, 1/r}`, `r ≠ ±1`:
`2μ f(r) f(1/r)`, signature `(1,1)`; quadruple: `4μ Re(f(z) f(1/z))`, a real form on
`ℂ² ≅ ℝ⁴` of signature `(2,2)`. ∎

So, exactly as for ζ (Bombieri 2000, Thm 8: the negative index of a large enough
truncation of Weil's form counts the off-line zeros), the curve Weil form's negative
index counts off-circle zeros — and here it is computable from point counts alone.

**Proposition 2 (Cited; Weil, via Hallouin–Perret).** For a real curve, `M` is the
Gram matrix of explicit divisor classes: with `Γ^k` the graph of the `k`-th power of
Frobenius on `C × C` and `p` the projection onto the orthogonal complement `E` of the
horizontal and vertical classes in `NS(C×C)_ℝ`,
`⟨p(Γ^k), p(Γ^{k+i})⟩ = q^k (q^i + 1 − N_i)` for the pairing `⟨γ, γ'⟩ = −γ·γ'`, which
the Hodge index theorem makes positive definite on `E` (Hallouin–Perret,
arXiv:1409.2357, Thm 1, Lemma 3, eq. (5); Lemma 12: every Gram minor `≥ 0`).

**Every rung of the target ladder exists for curves, and is finite:**

| rung | curves |
|---|---|
| local finite + archimedean data | `P_d` (closed points by degree); no archimedean place |
| explicit host-aware global form | `W_n` / `M`, each entry split into pole terms and place terms (table above) |
| controlled finite restrictions | polynomials of degree `< n` |
| independently constructed positive forms `B_n^*B_n` | Gram matrices of `p(Γ^0), …, p(Γ^{n−1})` under `−(intersection)`: built from geometry, not from the zeros |
| uniform signed residual `ε_n` | **0** — Lemma 3 is an identity |
| dense/core closure | unnecessary: the zero set is finite, `n = 2g` already decides (Prop. 1) |
| global positivity criterion | RH for curves (Weil) |

The decisive features are **finiteness** (`dim H^1 = 2g`; ζ_C is periodic in `s`
with finitely many zeros per period) and the **host ascent `C ⇝ C × C`**, where the
Hodge index theorem supplies positivity that does not mention the zeros.

## 2. The number-field rung by rung

Bombieri's form of Weil's criterion (Bombieri 2000, §3 Thm 1–2, verified): RH holds
iff `T[f ∗ f^*] ≥ 0` for every `f ∈ C_c^∞((0,∞))`, where `T[f] = Σ_ρ f̂(ρ)` is given
by the explicit formula as pole terms minus `Σ_n Λ(n)(f(n) + f^*(n))` minus the
archimedean principal-value term. Host-aware split:

| term | host | status |
|---|---|---|
| `f̂(0) + f̂(1)` | global `Q` (the poles of `ζ`, the `H^0/H^2` analogues) | explicit |
| `Λ(p^m) f(p^m)`, `m = 1` | the local host `Q_p` | explicit; finitely many for compact support |
| `Λ(p^m) f(p^m)`, `m ≥ 2` | the same `Q_p` (prime powers) | explicit |
| archimedean term `W_ℝ` | the **mandatory** point `∞` (no integral default; Γ-factor) | explicit; delicate principal value |

**The natural controlled restriction is a host ascent, not a dimension cut.** Test
functions supported in `[1/X, X]` see exactly the primes `p ≤ X`: the form lives on
the S-assembly with `S = {∞} ∪ {p ≤ X}` (A's `ASSEMBLY(S)` host). Enlarging `X` is a
host ascent that *adds terms* — positivity in the smaller host is "local
completeness inside a host", which (as A's firewall says) does not survive ascent.
The only window where positivity is **proved** is the smallest one: support in
`[2^{−1/2}, 2^{1/2}]` (multiplicative), where no prime contributes and only the
archimedean and pole terms remain (Yoshida 1992; Connes–Consani 2021, Thm 1 and
Thm 6.11; Bombieri 2000, Thm 12 for sufficiently small intervals; beyond the
interval the archimedean functional alone is not positive, CC §3 Rem. 3.9(ii)).

**Rung-by-rung status for the completed Weil form:**

| rung | what is needed | status |
|---|---|---|
| test-function space | a declared space, e.g. `C_c^∞((0,∞))` in Bombieri's normalisation | available (cited) |
| topology / common form core | a topology in which the quadratic form is lower semicontinuous on a core that the host ascent exhausts | **Open** for the full form; C's bounded theorem does not apply as stated, because `T[f∗f^*]` is not a bounded form on `L²(d^×x)` |
| finite-prime contributions | `Λ(p) f(p)` with host `Q_p` | explicit |
| prime-power contributions | `Λ(p^m) f(p^m)`, `m ≥ 2` | explicit |
| archimedean term | `W_ℝ`, principal value, mandatory point | explicit; positivity only in the smallest window (cited) |
| pole/completion terms | `f̂(0) + f̂(1)` | explicit |
| Fourier/Mellin conventions | one declared unitary convention (C §1: `H_σ = L²((0,∞), x^{2σ} dx/x)`, `σ = 1/2`) | declared in C; must be carried unchanged through every term |
| boundedness / closability or replacement | C's Theorem needs a bounded `W`, or a proved substitute on a common form core | **Open** |
| dense exhaustion | supports `[1/X, X]`, `X → ∞`, i.e. the S-assemblies exhaust `A_Q` | available as sets; the topology above is the open part |
| `B_n` from independent arithmetic | positive forms built **without** the zeros whose Gram entries reproduce the prime side — the analogue of `p(Γ^k)` | **Open** — this is the core missing object |
| uniform signed residual `ε_n → 0` | `|W(f,f) − ‖B_n f‖²| ≤ ε_n ‖f‖²` uniformly on the `n`-th host | **Open** |
| passage to the completed form | from the S-local forms to `Λ(s) = Λ(1−s)`'s global form | **Open** |

C's theorem (bounded Hermitian comparison: nested dense `H_n`, `|W − ‖B_n·‖²| ≤ ε_n`,
`ε_n → 0` ⇒ `W ⪰ 0`) is a sufficient condition whose entire content is in its
hypotheses: if `W ⪰ 0` one may take `B_n = W^{1/2}` with `ε_n = 0`, so the theorem
certifies nothing unless the `B_n` are built from independent information. Defining
`B_n` by factorising a matrix already known to be positive (Cholesky of a truncation
that was checked PSD) would put the answer into the construction.

## 3. The attempted transfer, and exactly where it breaks

**Attempt.** Copy the curve proof: (i) write the prime side as a Lefschetz trace of
a "Frobenius" on a space `H`; (ii) realise the Weil form entries as intersection
numbers of explicit classes `p(Γ^λ)` on a square `X × X`; (iii) get positivity from a
Hodge-index theorem on that square; (iv) conclude by Prop. 1 at finite size.

| step | curves | `Spec Z` | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| (i) trace formula | Lefschetz for Frobenius on `H^1`, `dim 2g` | Connes' trace formula for the scaling action on the adele class space `A_Q/Q^×` — an assembly-type host; the "Frobenius" is a flow `ℝ_+^×`, not an endomorphism `ℤ`, and the space is infinite-dimensional | survives, with an infinite-dimensional host |
| (ii) Gram identity | Hallouin–Perret Lemma 3: an identity on `NS(C×C)` | needs a host for "the square of `Spec Z`" carrying an intersection pairing | **not supplied in this dossier:** candidate hosts for a compactified `Spec Z` now exist (Connes–Consani 2026, §4.5), but none is verified *here* to carry the Gram/intersection identity the curve proof uses |
| (iii) positivity | Hodge index on `C × C` | Connes–Consani: the missing step is a Riemann–Roch / `H^1` on the square of the Scaling Site (arXiv:1805.10501); an *independent* self-adjoint prime-built operator family now exists (Zeta Spectral Triples, 2026) but its spectral convergence is conjectural | **Open** (no proven Hodge-index-type positivity) |
| (iv) finite decision | `n = 2g` suffices | the zero set is infinite; Bombieri Thm 8: truncations reveal off-line zeros only when large enough, so no a priori finite size | **breaks** (no a priori finite size) |

**Where the transfer first breaks, and what stays open above it.** This particular
curve/Hodge-index transfer first encounters its *geometric* bottleneck at the
square-host ascent: curves pass from `C` to the richer host `C × C`, where positivity
is a theorem about geometry; for `Spec Z` no construction verified in this dossier
supplies that square host together with the intersection pairing the Gram identity
needs. This is **one** obstruction among several, not the only one: the analytic
rungs of §2 — a common form core / closability (so `C`'s bounded comparison theorem
applies), an independent `B_n` built without the zeros, a uniform signed residual
`ε_n → 0`, and a finite-or-limiting decision — remain **independently open** even if a
square host were handed over. In the language of A, the arrow `Spec Z ⇝ (Spec Z)²` is
an `EMBED`-type host arrow with no verifier this dossier can discharge; the curve
case's arrow passes because `C × C` is a scheme with an intersection theory.

## 4.5 Current literature (2024–2026) vs. the missing structures

Surveyed in the consolidation round against the rungs of §2–§3. Each entry is judged
only by which of our missing structures it is *verified here* to supply — a square-like
host, an intersection pairing, a Hodge-index-type positivity theorem, an independently
constructed positive Gram/operator family, a trace identity matching the complete Weil
form, a common topology/core, a uniform residual, or a limiting/global passage.

| work | supplies, for our transport | does **not** (yet) supply |
|---|---|---|
| Connes–Consani–Moscovici, *Zeta zeros and prolate wave operators* (arXiv:2310.18423, v2 2024) — the **semilocal/Sonin** construction is part of this same paper, not a separate extension | a functional-analytic core for a **finite set `S` of places** — exactly an `S`-assembly host in A's vocabulary; prolate positive spectrum ↔ low zeros, Sonin space ↔ UV | the Gram/intersection identity or a Hodge-index positivity for the *complete* Weil form |
| Connes–Consani–Moscovici, *Zeta Spectral Triples* (arXiv:2511.22755, Nov 2025) | an **independently constructed self-adjoint operator family** — rank-one perturbations of the scaling spectral triple built from Euler products over `p ≤ λ²`; self-adjointness **proven** via an extension of the Carathéodory–Fejér theorem for **Toeplitz** matrices (same genus as our Toeplitz `M`). The closest thing yet to a legitimate independent `B_n`: positivity is **not** obtained by factoring a known-positive matrix | a **proof** that the spectra converge to the zeros: convergence as `N, λ → ∞` is numerical/**conjectural** (a rigorous proof would give RH). This is precisely our open *limiting-passage* rung |
| Connes–Consani, *On the Absolute Geometry of `Spec Z`* (arXiv:2606.06604, 2026) | a candidate **host**: the absolute `F₁`-arithmetic curve `(Spec Z)_{F₁}`, a common geometric origin (p-adic Hodge, complex-analytic, adelic scaling) | a self-product/"square", an intersection pairing, or any positivity theorem (none in the abstract; RH not mentioned) |
| Connes–Consani, *The Absolute Twistor Line and the Geometry of `\overline{Spec Z}`* (arXiv:2609.00299, 2026) | a candidate **archimedean/compactification host**: `\overline{Spec Z}` as the affine absolute curve amalgamated with an archimedean component over `F_{1²}`, the archimedean place as an absolute twistor line | the intersection pairing + Hodge-index positivity on the square |
| Connes–Consani, *On the Jacobian of `\overline{Spec Z}`* (arXiv:2602.15941, 2026; forthcoming J. Noncomm. Geom.) | announces a Jacobian for the compactified `\overline{Spec Z}` | **not independently read in this round** — its internal construction (whether it carries a pairing/positivity) is `UNVERIFIED` here; cited by title + arXiv id, not characterized |

**Net.** As of 2026 the *host* side has moved: several candidate geometric hosts for a
compactified `Spec Z` now exist, and an independent self-adjoint prime-built operator
family exists whose positivity is not tautological. What is still missing — and what no
cited construction is verified here to provide — is the **combination** the curve proof
needs: a square host **with** a proven intersection/Gram identity **and** a
Hodge-index-type positivity, **plus** the analytic passage to the limit. These are
distinct open rungs (§2); current work advances some without closing the stack.
